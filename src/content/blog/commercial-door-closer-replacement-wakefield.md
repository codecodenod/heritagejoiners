Not every commercial maintenance job is a big one. Doors that slam, doors that will not latch, a handrail that has worked loose — none of it is dramatic, but in a working office it is a daily nuisance, and it is the sort of thing that gets left because it never quite justifies calling anyone out.

Heritage Joiners was called in by [Spawforths](https://spawforths.co.uk/), a firm of planners, architects and masterplanners in East Ardsley near Wakefield.

## The problem with the office doors

Three of the overhead door closers had failed, and on all three it was the latch action that had gone.

A closer does two separate jobs, with a separate adjustment for each. The closing speed brings the door most of the way across, under control. Then for the last four inches or so the latch speed takes over, and that is what gives the door the final push to engage the latch properly.

On these three the latch adjustment had stopped doing anything at all. Wind it in, wind it out, no difference — the doors came across and then slammed into the frame every time. No amount of adjusting was going to bring them back. Once that side of a closer has gone, it has gone: you are replacing the unit, not tuning it.

## Replacing and setting up the door closers

All three were replaced and adjusted so each door closed at a controlled speed and latched every time.

Setting them up is the part that takes the time. Closing speed set too hard and the door still slams, even on a new unit. Set it too soft and the door drifts to a stop short of the frame and sits there, which is worse than useless on a door meant to stay shut. Then the latch speed on top of that, so it engages cleanly without a bang.
